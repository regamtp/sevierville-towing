Roads Where Sevierville Calls Start

Sevierville is the county seat of Sevier County, and the roads that produce a tow do not behave like one another. The name on the sign is the first useful fact.

Winfield Dunn Parkway and I-40 Exit 407

State Route 66 is signed Winfield Dunn Parkway. It runs about 8.6 miles from I-40 Exit 407 south to Main Street, and that whole route sits inside the Sevierville city limits. At Main Street it meets US-441 and US-411. Exit 407 is a diverging diamond. TDOT opened that pattern to traffic on June 30, 2015, and the lanes cross to the left side of the bridge. TDOT told drivers to pay close attention to the signs and the pavement markings while they got used to the crossover. If you are on a ramp, name the ramp and the direction: I-40 toward Knoxville, I-40 toward Newport, or SR-66 toward town or back toward the interstate. South of the interchange the signals on this road are numbered. The City of Sevierville marks the convention-center turn as signal 17.4, onto Gists Creek Road. The Sevierville Convention Center is at 202 Gists Creek Road, and the city notes that the north end overlooks the Sevierville Golf Club. A stall in that turn lane is a different hookup from a stall in the convention-center lot. The outlet and hotel rows are on this parkway. The courthouse end of the same road is downtown, not the outlet corridor. The layout is on the Highway 66 page, and the convention-center turn has its own Gists Creek page.

The Parkway toward the mountains

South of the Main Street junction, US-441 uses Forks of the River Parkway around the west side of downtown and continues as the Great Smoky Mountains Parkway. That is the road people mean by the Parkway on the way toward Pigeon Forge. Between Pigeon Forge and Gatlinburg, the divided section is the Foothills Parkway Spur, usually called the Spur, and US-441 is the Parkway again through Gatlinburg. Inside Great Smoky Mountains National Park the same highway is Newfound Gap Road. A car that dies in a through lane here is in tourist traffic. Name a business or a traffic light, and say whether you are still on the Sevierville end, on the Spur, or already in Gatlinburg.

Chapman Highway toward Knoxville

West of Main Street, US-441 and US-411 share Chapman Highway through Boyd's Creek to Seymour, on the Sevier County and Blount County line. At Seymour, US-411 turns off at Boyds Creek Highway, SR-338, and US-441 keeps the Chapman Highway name toward Knoxville. This is a commuter road, and the shoulders are thinner than on the divided parkway of Highway 66. Give the direction, toward Knoxville or toward Sevierville, and the nearest school, store, or crossroad. Seymour and Boyds Creek each have a page because the landmarks change along this stretch.

Dolly Parton Parkway

East of the square, US-411 is Dolly Parton Parkway, the road toward Newport rather than the road to I-40. It passes the Sevier County High School side of town and the north end of Veterans Boulevard. The Gatlinburg-Pigeon Forge Airport is in Sevierville, on this east side of town, not out at the interstate. Farther east the highway is signed Newport Highway, through Cherokee Hills and on toward Fairgarden. A stall here needs the cross street and the direction, toward downtown or toward Newport. The Dolly Parton Parkway page and the airport page split those stops.

Veterans Boulevard

State Route 449, Veterans Boulevard, opened to traffic in 2006 as a bypass of the Parkway. The route runs about 6.2 miles from Dolly Parton Parkway in Sevierville south to US-441 in Pigeon Forge. In Sevierville it crosses Middle Creek and meets Collier Drive, which cuts back to the Parkway. Parts of the bypass follow the old Middle Creek Road alignment, so a map pin can say Middle Creek while you are on a divided highway. LeConte Medical Center is not on the bypass. The emergency department is at 742 Middle Creek Road. If you are in that lot, say Middle Creek Road. If you are on the multi-lane highway and can see the creek, you are on Veterans Boulevard. Use the Veterans Boulevard page for the bypass and the Middle Creek page for the hospital road.

Cabins, Campgrounds, and Parking Lots

A lot of Sevierville Towing calls never touch a highway lane. The vehicle is up a rental drive, boxed into a campsite, or sitting in a hotel row.

Wears Valley Road is US-321 from the Parkway west toward Townsend. Cabin drives and campground lanes turn off it. The highway itself is narrower than the Parkway, and the spot where the vehicle is actually sitting is often a gravel drive, a slope, or a pad shared with other guests' cars. The east side of the county has the same kind of road. Pittman Center Road, SR-416, leaves US-411 in eastern Sevierville and runs south as a two-lane road through hilly ground to US-321 at Pittman Center. Caton's Chapel is in that foothill country. Before a truck starts up one of those drives, say whether the last stretch is paved, how steep it is, and whether a full-size truck can turn around without backing the length of the road. Mountain towing is the winch work for a steep hollow or a vehicle off the pavement. A flat retail lot does not need that setup. The Wears Valley and Caton's Chapel pages cover those two foothill areas.

Motorhomes and trailers come off I-40 onto SR-66 through the year, headed for campgrounds in the county. A passenger-car truck is the wrong tool for a motorhome, a fifth-wheel, or a travel trailer. Say which one it is, about how long it is, and whether it still rolls and steers. Those same facts matter in a campground lane, where trees and the neighboring site can block the approach. Camper and RV towing is the service for that rig.

Hotel lots and the retail rows along Winfield Dunn Parkway produce a quieter set of problems: a battery that died after the car sat while people were gone for the day, keys locked in a rental, a flat in a parking row, or a vehicle a property manager wants removed. Private-property towing happens when the owner or the manager asks for the removal. It is not a sweep of a lot. Jump starts, lockouts, fuel delivery, and flat-tire changes are separate jobs from a tow.

If the vehicle is already inside the national park, say that before anything else. The National Park Service prohibits commercial vehicles on Newfound Gap Road, and a vehicle inside the park is not moved without park authorization. A breakdown on the town roads below the boundary is an ordinary call. A breakdown past the entrance is not.

What to Do While You Wait

If anyone is hurt, call 911 before you call for a tow. For a trooper anywhere in Tennessee, *THP (*847) reaches the nearest Tennessee Highway Patrol dispatch. The tow line is (865) 277-9406, and it is for the truck, not for an ambulance. LeConte Medical Center's emergency department is at 742 Middle Creek Road in Sevierville.

If the vehicle still rolls and you can move it without driving into traffic, get it off the travel lane. The Tennessee Highway Patrol tells drivers to steer onto the right shoulder when that is safe. If moving right would put you in worse traffic, use an emergency lane, the median, or another spot clear of the lane. Skip a bridge deck, and skip a spot jammed against a guardrail, when another pull-off is open. Once you are on the shoulder, turn the front wheels to the right so a hit is more likely to push the car farther off the road, and turn the hazard lights on.

On a divided, controlled-access highway or an interstate, including I-40, Tennessee Code Annotated 55-10-117 is more specific. When a crash has no apparent serious injury or death, and the vehicle can still be driven safely under its own power without further damage, the driver should move it off the roadway onto a shoulder, an emergency lane, a median, or another safe spot. A driver who does that before an officer arrives is not considered at fault for the crash solely because the vehicle was moved. The statute is limited to those highways. It does not cover a signalized road such as Chapman Highway, and it does not apply when the vehicle will not drive and needs a tow. In that case, leave it where it is and call.

On a blowout, do not slam the brakes. Hold the wheel, ease off the gas, signal if you have to change lanes, and coast toward the shoulder or the next exit. Put the hazards on after you stop.

Where you stand depends on the road. If you are well off the pavement and traffic is light, staying in the vehicle is the steadier choice. If traffic is heavy, as it is on the Parkway and on Winfield Dunn Parkway on a busy afternoon, get out on the side away from the lanes and wait several feet off the roadway. Do not stand in front of, behind, or beside the vehicle where traffic can reach you. Keep the phone on. The driver may call to confirm a ramp, a signal number, or which side of a divided road you are on.

Tennessee's Move Over law, Tennessee Code Annotated 55-8-132, tells other drivers what to do when they approach a stopped tow truck with its lights flashing. TDOT's description of the law includes recovery vehicles: move over a lane when that is safe, or slow down when a lane change is not. TDOT states the penalty can be a fine of up to $500 and up to 30 days in jail. That duty belongs to the traffic going by. Your part is to stay out of the lane.

A lockout is a different wait. Stay with the vehicle so the driver can confirm it is yours. If a child or a pet is inside, or the engine is running, say that in the first sentence. Do not pry the door frame. A bent door turns a lockout into body work. For fuel, say whether the engine takes gasoline or diesel. The fuel delivery on this site is enough to reach a station. For a flat, say whether a usable spare is in the vehicle. If the shoulder is too narrow to work on, the stop becomes a tow to a lot. Chapman Highway through Seymour is a common place where that choice comes up.

What to Have Ready When You Call

You do not need a membership. You do need a location a driver can find without circling.

  • The road and the direction. South on the Parkway and south on Chapman Highway are opposite sides of Sevierville.
  • A landmark you can see: an exit ramp, a numbered signal, a mile marker, a business, a creek, or a cross street. Exit 407, signal 17.4, Collier Drive, and the high school are more useful than "near Sevierville."
  • The vehicle: a car, a truck, a motorcycle, a motorhome, a fifth-wheel, or a travel trailer. For a camper, the approximate length. Year and make help when you know them.
  • Whether it rolls and steers, and whether it is in a lane, on a shoulder, in a ditch, or in a lot.
  • Where it should go, if you already know: a shop, a hotel, a cabin, or a house. Long-distance towing on this site, including a haul to a shop in Knoxville, is quoted before the vehicle is loaded.
  • A phone number that works at the spot where you are standing.

The form higher on this page is for questions that are not an active breakdown. If the vehicle is stopped in a lane or on a narrow shoulder, call.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast can you get here?

Response time depends on where you are and what's happening on the roads, but because we're local to Sevierville, we're usually close by. Call and we'll give you a realistic time the moment we know your location.

Do you tow at night and on weekends?

Yes. We run 24 hours a day, seven days a week, 365 days a year. Breakdowns don't keep business hours, and neither do we.

What kinds of vehicles can you tow?

Cars, trucks, SUVs, motorcycles, RVs, and larger equipment. If you're not sure we can handle your vehicle, call and describe it — we'll tell you straight.

Will you tell me the price before you start?

Yes. We give you an upfront price based on your location, your vehicle, and where it's going. No surprise charges once the job is done.

Which Parkway am I on?

South of downtown, the Parkway is US-441, the Great Smoky Mountains Parkway, toward Pigeon Forge. Winfield Dunn Parkway is SR-66 from Main Street north to I-40 Exit 407. Dolly Parton Parkway is US-411 east of the square. Veterans Boulevard is SR-449, the bypass. Chapman Highway is US-441 toward Knoxville.

What should I say if I am stopped at Exit 407?

Name the ramp and the direction. That is I-40 toward Knoxville, I-40 toward Newport, or SR-66 toward Sevierville or back toward the interstate. Exit 407 is a diverging diamond, so the lanes cross to the left side of the bridge. On the bridge is not a precise enough location.

Can you tow a motorhome or a travel trailer?

Yes. Camper and RV towing is one of the services on this site. Say whether it is a motorhome, a fifth-wheel, or a travel trailer, about how long it is, and whether it still rolls and steers. The same details matter in a campground lane.

What if I only need fuel, a jump, a spare, or a lockout?

Those are separate from a tow. For fuel, say gasoline or diesel. For a flat, say whether a usable spare is in the vehicle. For a lockout, stay with the car, and say at the start of the call if a child, a pet, or a running engine is inside.

The driveway is steep. What should I tell you?

Say whether the surface is paved or gravel, how steep the last stretch is, and whether a full-size truck can turn around. That is the usual question on cabin drives off Wears Valley Road and along Pittman Center Road.

What if the vehicle is inside the national park?

Say so before anything else. The National Park Service prohibits commercial vehicles on Newfound Gap Road, and a vehicle inside Great Smoky Mountains National Park is not moved without park authorization. A breakdown on the town roads below the entrance is a different call.

Can you remove a car from a private lot?

Private-property towing happens when the owner or the manager requests the removal. Have that person's name, a phone number that works while the driver is on the lot, and the row or space where the vehicle sits.
